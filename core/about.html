About

In the 4th grade, we were tasked with picking stocks and updating their prices on a large hand-written chart. Each day started with a lot of excitement as we looked up our prices and calculated our imaginary balances. I was so intrigued with it all — the numbers, the data, the charts — that I started going to the library during recess to research stocks. I also started saving my lunch money so that I could buy penny stocks.

Learning Excel also fascinated me. Seeing everything I had previously calculated by pencil and paper being instantly computed felt like magic. This expanded to going to the computer lab after school to learn how to create basic websites with the help of the teacher and other students.

After studying economics in college, I moved to NYC to work in finance and then spent some time working in fundraising for my alma mater. I had always been referred to as the Excel-guy and found myself drawn to the data aspects of both finance and fundraising roles.

While creating reports, I would investigate discrepancies and figure out how to make the data more reliable in order to make the reporting process more seamless. Most issues I explored left me wanting to look further than my role permitted. So I eventually shifted to data-centric roles and took on validation, migration, ETL, integration, and modeling.
